# From Ideas to Innovation My SGX3 FacultyHack Experience

*Antigone Anthony*

Participating in the SGX3 FacultyHack was a meaningful experience. As a college biology instructor, I am always looking for ways to help students understand concepts. SGX3 allowed me to collaborate with educators while developing a project for my Anatomy and Physiology laboratory students.

Working with my mentor, Dr. Ahmad Al-Omari from Our Lady of the Lake University, was part of the experience. His guidance helped me focus on my students' learning needs before selecting tools. I explored how BioDigital Human, Jupyter, NAIRR, and TACC-supported technologies could improve activities. BioDigital Human was useful because its three-dimensional models allow students to rotate structures, isolate organs, explore body systems, and understand anatomical relationships.

The project encourages students to move beyond memorization. Students can locate a structure, examine nearby structures, explain how its position supports its function, and apply their knowledge to a clinical scenario. I also revised my laboratory syllabus using Bloom's Taxonomy and included guidance for the responsible use of artificial intelligence.

Presenting during the SGX3 poster presentation was a highlight of the experience. It was a great opportunity to share my work, explain how it could benefit my students, and receive feedback. I enjoyed discussing my ideas with educators interested in improving science education through technology. The presentation increased my confidence and showed how much my project had developed.

Overall, SGX3 helped me grow as an educator and researcher. I left with new resources, stronger ideas, and a clearer vision for using accessible technology to support biology students at an HBCU.
